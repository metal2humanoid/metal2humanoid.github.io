How to sponsor·Metal2Humanoid, proposed MIT IAP 2027

Help students build humanoid robots from the ground up.

Metal2Humanoid is a proposed non-credit MIT IAP 2027 course in which 20 students, in six teams, each build an actuator, assemble and commission a custom humanoid, develop robot skills, and present both the Berkeley Humanoid Lite and the Metal2Humanoid at a public showcase on January 29, 2027. The course requests no departmental funding: the organizing team is arranging hardware and materials through sponsors and parts donors, and the robot design and course materials are released openly.

What support makes possible

  • 20 student-built actuators

    An individual actuator kit for every student (motor, cycloidal gearbox parts, bearings, encoder, motor controller, wiring), kept afterwards as reusable teaching modules.

  • Two humanoid platforms

    A Berkeley Humanoid Lite that walks on student-built actuators in Week 1, and the Metal2Humanoid, a larger custom humanoid (provisionally 26 actuators) assembled by the teams in Week 2.

  • A public showcase and open materials

    Demonstrations of both robots on January 29 with an invited audience, plus openly released design files, lab guides, and notebooks that other groups can repeat.

What we need

In-kind hardware is the most useful form of support: actuators and motors above all, then compute, printers and fabrication, tools and test equipment. Financial support covers what in-kind contributions do not, such as kit preparation, consumables, spares, and shipping. Quantities below follow the current course plan and are confirmed once the designs are frozen.

  1. Actuators, motors, encoders, and sensors

    Actuators or motor-plus-gearbox modules for 20 individual student kits and for the Metal2Humanoid's provisional 26 joints, plus 10–20% spares of failure-prone parts; magnetic encoders, IMUs, and sensors.

  2. Batteries, electronics, compute, and motor controllers

    Battery packs and management, power distribution, motor controller boards, CAN interfaces, on-board compute, and shared GPU or cloud compute for simulation and policy training.

  3. Fabrication, machining, printing, and materials

    3D printing capacity and filament, laser-cut or machined structural and interface parts, fasteners, bearings, and stock for 20 actuator kits and the humanoid structure.

  4. Tools, test equipment, spare parts, and consumables

    Six team workstations, four to eight protected actuator testing stations, bench power supplies, multimeters, soldering stations, hand tools, robot support fixtures, cabling, and connectors.

  5. Financial support

    Funds for components, kit preparation, and replacement parts, used where in-kind contributions do not cover a need.

  6. Technical mentorship and engineering support

    Review of designs and lab procedures, help with motor control and firmware, and occasional guest participation in labs.

Where support goes

The current course plan calls for three hardware kits and shared teaching equipment. Final quantities are confirmed against the frozen designs.

  • Kit A, individual actuators: 20 kits with motor, cycloidal gearbox components, bearings, spacers and shafts, housing and fasteners, encoder, motor controller, wiring, a parts tray, and an assembly guide, plus 10–20% spares of failure-prone parts.
  • Kit B, Berkeley Humanoid Lite demonstration: prepared BHL-compatible structures that reuse student-built actuators, controller and communication interfaces, power supply, sensors, a walking support fixture, and a validated gait controller.
  • Kit C, the Metal2Humanoid: provisionally 26 installed actuators (six hip, two waist, two neck, six in the legs, ten in the arms) with three to six spares, prefabricated structural parts, central electronics, power distribution, compute, sensors, and a support fixture.
  • Shared teaching equipment: six team workstations, 20 assembly trays, four to eight testing stations, tools, soldering and electrical test equipment, 3D printers and workshop supplies, safety fixtures, and shared GPU or compute for simulation.
  • Documentation and open release of the robot design, lab guides, and course materials.

What sponsors receive

We keep recognition straightforward and tied to real use of what you provide.

  • Recognition on this website, in the course materials, and at the public showcase on January 29, 2027.
  • Credited hardware demonstrations and build updates that show your components in use, on student-built actuators and on both humanoids.
  • Invitations to the showcase and opportunities to engage with student and independent robotics builders.
  • Practical feedback from hands-on use of your products by 20 students over four weeks.
  • Participation in a growing open robotics education community, with the design and materials released openly.

How Big Companies Fit In

Open robotics education and the companies that make robotics hardware need each other. The relationship works best when each side contributes what it is good at.

Companies contribute

  • Components and development kits
  • Manufacturing and design-for-manufacturing expertise
  • Tools, test equipment, and compute
  • Engineering time and resources

The community contributes

  • Experimentation with those technologies in real builds
  • Open documentation that others can follow
  • Honest, practical feedback from hands-on use
  • People who learn to build with those technologies

For an actuator or motor manufacturer, the partnership can go further: the Metal2Humanoid's actuator selection is being finalized now, before the design freeze, so a partner's actuators can be designed in from the start, with published assembly guides, test data, student builds that use them, and direct feedback from a cohort learning on your hardware.
